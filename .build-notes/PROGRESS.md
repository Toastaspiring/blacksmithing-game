**Why round 3 exists.** The measurement half of the fix works: no ordinary slide
up, down or through the thickness changes a sword's difficulty. The hard part is a corner case. A player can copy a
part and nudge the copy by a hair, making two near-identical parts; the game then counts one of them and ignores the
other. Every rule for picking which one breaks something:
- M3's original rule can change the pick when the whole sword slides across its centre line.
- Round 1's rule let a hidden grip speck change it.
- Round 2's rule let a hidden duplicate change it, and mirroring the sword too.

So round 3 goes back to M3's rule for that pick, which hidden parts and mirroring can't disturb. The rare sliding case
gets written up for you with numbers. Then two reviewers, the Studio check, and the fix's own commit. If even that
review fails on this pick, I'll stop and hand you the near-copy question.

**One question for you stands out, when you're back.** Sliding a whole sword *sideways* still changes its difficulty,
because the spec measures symmetry and the edge from a fixed centre line. One default arrow-key step (0.05 stud) is
enough to turn the Starter Sword's Hammering and Sharpening from Normal to Hard. The finished sword looks the same.
- What the game does now: as the approved spec says (the centre line stays fixed).
- My recommendation: measure from the sword's own grip instead. On the sample swords that moved 93 designs a little
  and changed no tier. I'll write it up properly with the other decisions (O12).
